# POLARIS — G1.3-E UNSUPPORTED & OVERSTATED CLAIMS AUDIT

## Executive Summary

This document details all claims in the G1.3-E research package that exceed available empirical evidence, use subjective evaluative language, conflate protocol specs with safety thresholds, or risk creating unverified engineering facts.

Consistent with audit policy, **no original research files were modified**. Instead, this document provides a formal **Required Correction Register** stating recommended neutral wording and corrective actions.

---

## 1. DETAILED ANALYSIS OF AUDIT FINDINGS

### 1.1 Evaluative & Subjective Language Overstatements
The research report currently uses terms such as *"scientifically superior"*, *"exemplary"*, *"complete failure-mode coverage"*, *"zero false confidence"*, and *"very low risk"* to describe Candidate Option E (Dynamic Uncertainty) and Option F (Hybrid).

- **Problem**: Comparative evaluative judgments require rigorous empirical benchmark testing, comparative HIL simulations, and formal error bounds. In the absence of empirical validation data on real polar hardware, such claims constitute unverified promotional language.
- **Corrective Policy**: Evaluative assertions must be converted to neutral, objective scientific descriptions, such as *"conceptually promising candidate for further investigation"* or *"conceptually thorough target architecture."*

### 1.2 Conflation of Protocol Polling Specs with Safety Freshness Limits
Section 1 of `G1.3E_MEASUREMENT_BY_MEASUREMENT_ANALYSIS.md` cites SCADA protocol polling specs (e.g., Modbus RTU polling rate, SAE J1939 CAN-bus broadcast rate) under *"Evidence Supporting Criterion."*

- **Problem**: A protocol communication polling interval or sensor update frequency is NOT a safety-freshness threshold or survivability validity period. A sensor updating every 10 seconds does not mean its reading remains valid for safety credit for 10 seconds (or 10 minutes).
- **Corrective Policy**: Reclassify protocol update rates as *communication transport characteristics*, not safety credit rules.

### 1.3 Synthetic Model vs. Real Nuolja Generator Fact Blur
The research package repeatedly references Caterpillar C9/C13 engine specifications and industrial diesel generator parameters.

- **Problem**: The current POLARIS generator model is synthetic. There is currently no validated Nuolja generator telemetry or freshness dataset. Presenting generic Caterpillar specifications alongside Nuolja survivability calculations risks confusing generic literature facts with real POLARIS engineering facts.
- **Corrective Policy**: Explicitly preserve the knowledge boundary: generic OEM specs are Class 1 reference literature, but Nuolja generator freshness rules require station-specific empirical calibration.

### 1.4 Dynamic Uncertainty Equation Pseudo-Rigor
The equation $\sigma_{E_{	ext{gen}}}(t_{	ext{now}}, \Delta t_{	ext{age}}) = \int_{0}^{6	ext{h}} \sigma_{P_{	ext{gen}}}(t; \Delta t_{	ext{age}}) \, dt \le \epsilon_{	ext{surv\_tolerance}}$ is presented in Section 5 of the main report.

- **Problem**: The report does not supply an open-loop variance growth model $rac{d\sigma^2}{dt}$, a fitted sensor noise parameter $\sigma_{	ext{meas}}$, an empirical variance dataset, or a closed formula for $\epsilon_{	ext{surv\_tolerance}}$. Presenting a differential integral equation without parameterization creates a false impression of mathematical completeness.
- **Corrective Policy**: Reclassify the equation as a *Conceptual Research Framework* rather than an established safety model.
