# POLARIS — G1.3-E FAILURE MODES AND EFFECTS ANALYSIS (FMEA) FOR STALE TELEMETRY

## Executive Summary

This report delivers a rigorous Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA) investigating how stale, unverified, or corrupted generator telemetry can impact POLARIS safety credit and microgrid survivability, fulfilling **Research Dimension 3**.

---

## 1. FMEA METHODOLOGY & HAZARD CLASSIFICATION

Each stale telemetry failure mode is classified into one of three consequence severity tiers:

*   **Class A Hazard (Critical Failure Hazard)**: Stale telemetry masks a condition that leads to immediate unserved critical load or catastrophic station power blackouts during battery depletion.
*   **Class B Hazard (Deficit / Energy Exhaustion Hazard)**: Stale telemetry causes inaccurate calculation of $E_{\text{gen, credible}}$, leading to premature fuel depletion or undetected energy deficit over the 6-hour survivability horizon.
*   **Class C Hazard (Economic / Operational Penalty)**: Stale telemetry causes minor economic suboptimality or unnecessary generator starts without threatening critical load survivability.

## 3. DETAILED FAILURE MODE ANALYSIS

### 3.1 FM1: Stale Health Status Masking Active Engine Trip
- **Failure Mechanism**: A running generator ($u=1$) experiences an electrical fault (e.g., generator differential protection trip) or mechanical failure (e.g., low oil pressure trip). The generator breaker trips open immediately. Telemetry transmission fails or freezes.
- **Stale Signal**: Generator breaker state ($52a$) reports `CLOSED`, engine status reports `RUNNING`.
- **False Assumption Created**: POLARIS assumes $P_{\text{gen, credible}}(t) = P_{\text{rated}}$ and credits $69.6\text{ kWh}$ of survivability energy over the 6-hour horizon.
- **Survivability Impact**: If battery SOC is near the $20\%$ physical minimum ($SOC_{\text{min}}$), battery cannot supply the $11.6\text{ kW}$ critical load. Unserved critical load occurs immediately.
- **Corroborating Signal**: Station AC bus frequency drop, battery discharge current spike, or building bus voltage dip.
- **Freshness Control Requirement**: Hardened SCADA heartbeat ($< 1.0\text{ s}$) + bus frequency corroboration.

---

### 3.2 FM2: Stale Fuel Level Masking Fuel Line Rupture / Leak
- **Failure Mechanism**: A fuel line leak or transfer pump valve failure causes day-tank fuel to drain rapidly at $50\text{ L/h}$. The fuel level telemetry transmitter freezes or delays update.
- **Stale Signal**: Day-tank fuel level sensor reports $90\%$ capacity ($450\text{ L}$).
- **False Assumption Created**: POLARIS assumes fuel is sufficient for $> 12\text{ hours}$ of generation ($E_{\text{fuel, usable}} \gg E_{\text{critical}}$).
- **Survivability Impact**: Generator starves of fuel after $1.5\text{ hours}$. If battery energy is depleted, station enters emergency dark start condition.
- **Corroborating Signal**: Mass balance discrepancy ($\text{Fuel Level Drop Rate} \gg P_{\text{gen}} \cdot \text{BSFC}$) or fuel sump alarm bit.
- **Freshness Control Requirement**: Dynamic mass-balance validation check comparing fuel level derivative against active power output integral.

---

### 3.3 FM3: Stale Start-Readiness Masking Block Heater Failure (OFF Generator)
- **Failure Mechanism**: An OFF generator ($u=0$) relies on an electric block heater to maintain engine coolant at $40^\circ\text{C}$ in a $-40^\circ\text{C}$ polar environment. The block heater circuit breaker trips. The engine coolant temperature drops to $-20^\circ\text{C}$ over $2\text{ hours}$. Start-readiness telemetry is stale and reports $T_{\text{block}} = 40^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Stale Signal**: Engine block temperature RTD reports $40^\circ\text{C}$.
- **False Assumption Created**: POLARIS applies **G1.2** and credits the OFF generator with 6-hour survivability energy, assuming start-readiness is validated.
- **Survivability Impact**: When EMS commands generator start during battery depletion, the engine fails to crank due to gelled lube oil and frozen battery. Critical load service fails.
- **Corroborating Signal**: Block heater current draw ($I_{\text{heater}} > 0$), starter battery voltage under load, or engine bay ambient RTD.
- **Freshness Control Requirement**: Periodic active block heater current check + coolant temperature freshness rule before granting OFF generator credit.

---

### 3.4 FM4: Stale Active Power Masking Air Filter Clogging
- **Failure Mechanism**: Heavy polar blizzard snow clogs generator combustion air intake filters. Engine air-to-fuel ratio drops, causing engine thermal derating and power drop from $50\text{ kW}$ to $20\text{ kW}$. Power telemetry update hangs.
- **Stale Signal**: Generator power meter reports $50\text{ kW}$.
- **False Assumption Created**: POLARIS assumes generator is supplying total station load plus battery charging.
- **Survivability Impact**: Battery continues discharging to cover the unmeasured $30\text{ kW}$ deficit, depleting battery reserves unexpectedly.
- **Corroborating Signal**: Engine intake manifold pressure (boost pressure), exhaust gas temperature (EGT), or station bus frequency droop.
- **Freshness Control Requirement**: EGT/boost pressure sanity check combined with active power meter freshness bounds.

---

### 3.5 FM5: Frozen Telemetry Buffer (Stuck SCADA Payload)
- **Failure Mechanism**: Industrial SCADA gateway or Modbus TCP bridge encounters a software deadlock. The gateway continues responding to IP ping and TCP socket connections, but serves a frozen memory buffer containing stale telemetry values with updated local gateway timestamps.
- **Stale Signal**: All telemetry values (power, fuel, health) remain perfectly static over hours while packet timestamps update.
- **False Assumption Created**: POLARIS assumes the microgrid is operating in ideal steady-state.
- **Survivability Impact**: Conceals real-time dynamic failures across all sub-components.
- **Corroborating Signal**: Zero-variance check ($\text{Var}(X) = 0$ over $N$ samples) on naturally noisy analog measurements (such as AC voltage or active power).
- **Freshness Control Requirement**: Mandatory analog signal variance threshold (stuck-value detection) as a prerequisite for Stage 2 Structural Validity.

---

## 4. FMEA LESSONS & CONTROL SYSTEM MAPPING

1. **Zero-Variance Detection is Essential**: Timestamp freshness alone cannot catch stuck SCADA buffers. POLARIS must enforce non-zero variance on analog telemetry.
2. **Multi-Signal Corroboration Prevents False Credit**: No single measurement should grant generator safety credit without secondary corroboration (e.g., power + frequency, fuel level + flow).
3. **Decoupling Integrity Preserved**: Under all failure modes FM1–FM7, when freshness or validity fails, **Generator Credit evaluates to $0.0\text{ kWh}$**, and POLARIS evaluates remaining battery/renewable resources. Emergency is entered **ONLY** if remaining verified resources yield $M_{\text{surv}} < 0$.
