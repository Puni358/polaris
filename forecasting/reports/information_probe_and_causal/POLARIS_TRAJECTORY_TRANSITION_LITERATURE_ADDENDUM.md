# POLARIS trajectory-transition literature addendum

This review supplies external context only. It does not establish a POLARIS intervention or predictive gain.

- Carpentieri et al. (2023), *Intraday probabilistic forecasts of surface solar radiation with cloud scale-dependent autoregressive advection*, [Applied Energy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2023.121775): identifies cloud growth/dissipation omitted by advection-only systems as major uncertainty sources; scale-dependent evolution improved probabilistic skill. This is intraday satellite work, not a 24-hour NWP-only replication.
- Roy et al. (2022), *Impact of tropical convective conditions on solar irradiance forecasting based on cloud motion vectors*, [Environmental Research Letters](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ac94e6): finds CMV methods are strained when clouds cannot be treated as rigid advected bodies and connects greater convection with larger forecast error. Climate and lead range differ from POLARIS.
- *Skill and Skill Prediction of Cloud-Track Advection-Only Forecasting under a Cumulus-Dominated Regime* (2017), [JAMC](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/apme/56/3/jamc-d-16-0224.1.xml): reports short advection-only skill for cumulus-scale features and says decay is cloud-regime dependent. This supports the physical plausibility of transition difficulty, not the performance of any sequence model.
- Yang et al. (2016), *Regime-Dependent Short-Range Solar Irradiance Forecasting*, [JAMC](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/apme/55/7/jamc-d-15-0354.1.xml): demonstrates regime-dependent results at 15–180 min, but also documents overfitting risk in smaller regime subsets. It supports strict pre-registration rather than regime hunting.
- *Evaluation of downward and upward solar irradiances simulated by the IFS using airborne observations above Arctic low-level clouds* (2024), [Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/4157/2024/): describes cloud formation/dissipation and low-temperature liquid/ice representation as continuing Arctic NWP uncertainties. It is Arctic physical-process evidence, not a Nuolja forecast evaluation.
- Bentsen et al. (2024), *Deep and Probabilistic Solar Irradiance Forecast at the Arctic Circle*, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07806): reports an Arctic-Circle forecasting setting and point-forecast versus uncertainty-calibration trade-off. The abstract does not isolate transition timing.

## Synthesis

Literature supports the plausibility that cloud evolution, growth/dissipation, and regime transitions can degrade solar forecasts, and cautions that rigid advection and arbitrary regime models are insufficient. POLARIS contributes its own origin-level diagnostic: error was larger during/after transparently defined realised transmission transitions and complexity correlated more strongly with error than local H1-state density. This remains **association**, and cannot demonstrate that a sequence model will reduce MAE. The audit’s moderate-support classification therefore authorizes only a future *pre-registered design specification*, never training in this task.
