## Answers to the research questions

1. **Known phenomenon?** Analog-ensemble work operationalizes the idea that similar forecast states can map to a distribution of observed outcomes. The reviewed studies do not provide a standard, universal “state aliasing” statistic for solar forecasting.
2. **Horizon uncertainty growth?** Yes, particularly in intraday cloud-evolution work; the cited SolarSTEPS work explicitly attributes uncertainty to cloud growth/dissipation and finds simple advection insufficient. This is strongest at intraday scales, not direct proof for every H1–H24 POLARIS lead.
3. **Formation/dissipation vs advection?** Yes: SolarSTEPS explicitly identifies omitted cloud growth/dissipation as a major uncertainty source. This agrees with POLARIS’s failed rigid-translation diagnostic, but is mechanistic context rather than a replication.
4. **Are point observations sufficient?** The analog and cloud-motion literature motivates larger spatial representations/uncertainty ensembles, but no reviewed source proves a point observation is always insufficient.
5. **Does spatial NWP reduce ambiguity?** The spatiotemporal analog chapter reports benefit from spatial similarity. It does not imply every spatial NWP representation is useful; POLARIS tested several that were not robust.
6. **Arctic evidence?** The Arctic Circle preprint establishes directly relevant probabilistic-forecasting work, but the available abstract does not test state aliasing. Arctic-specific aliasing evidence is therefore **NOT IDENTIFIABLE from the reviewed sources**.
7. **Does POLARIS reproduce it?** No statistically defensible deterministic-solar-conditioned divergence-dominance horizon was found in the v2 audit. The appropriate POLARIS conclusion is **weak/inconclusive evidence**, not confirmation of the literature’s cloud-evolution mechanism.

## Separation of evidence

- **Literature evidence:** cloud evolution and its scale dependence create forecast uncertainty; analog ensembles use outcome spread among similar NWP states.
- **POLARIS empirical evidence:** after the prescribed deterministic-solar conditioning and FDR procedure, no divergence-dominance horizon met the pre-registered criterion.
- **Inference:** missing cloud-evolution information remains plausible, but this corrected aliasing test does not strengthen it to “moderate” or “strong” evidence.
